PROJECT 04

Rack, Cabling & Asset Plan

An operations-focused design package covering rack layout, device naming, port mapping, cable labels, asset records, power estimates, maintenance, and decommissioning.

Rack elevationCablingAsset inventoryPowerSOPsPlanned — update after completion

Objective

Create a complete deployment package for a small rack containing switches, servers, patch panels, and redundant power distribution. The project may be based on real lab equipment or a clearly labeled simulation.

Why this matters

Data center technicians stage, install, cable, inventory, repair, and remove equipment. A precise operations package demonstrates that you understand the work around the configuration—not just the commands.

Lab design

  • A 24U or 42U rack elevation with front and rear views.
  • Device naming convention, rack-unit placement, serial/asset fields, and ownership or purpose.
  • A-to-B power-feed plan and a simple wattage estimate with stated assumptions.
  • Copper and fiber port map with cable IDs, source, destination, type, length, and status.
  • Installation, maintenance, and decommission checklists.
  • A change plan including risk, rollback, validation, and communication steps.

Build sequence

  1. Define the rack requirements and constraints before placing equipment.
  2. Place heavy equipment low, reserve airflow space where required, and document manufacturer-specific assumptions.
  3. Create front and rear rack elevations and assign rack-unit positions.
  4. Build the asset inventory and cable schedule using the supplied templates.
  5. Estimate power demand and split dual-corded devices across A and B feeds.
  6. Write an installation method of procedure with pre-checks, execution, and validation.
  7. Create a failed-device replacement scenario and update the inventory and cable records.
  8. Write a secure decommission checklist covering data handling, labels, inventory, and disposal routing.

Acceptance tests

  • Every installed device appears in the rack elevation, inventory, power plan, and cable map.
  • Every cable ID has one source and one destination with no duplicates.
  • The planned load is below the stated power budget and assumptions are visible.
  • The deployment checklist includes safety, grounding/ESD awareness, change approval, and rollback.
  • A replacement can be performed using the documentation without guessing port assignments.

Implementation notes

Never claim electrical or mechanical engineering calculations beyond your training. Use equipment documentation, state all assumptions, and treat the power section as a basic planning exercise unless reviewed by a qualified professional.
